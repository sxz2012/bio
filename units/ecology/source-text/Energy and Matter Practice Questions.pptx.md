## Slide 4

Animals often shiver when they are cold. Explain this process in terms of energy and homeostasis.

## Slide 5


In order to increase the body temperature an animal will shiver to generate heat energy that raises their internal temperature and helps them maintain homeostasis.
Shivering generates heat because it involves quick muscle contractions that requires glucose to be converted into ATP through cellular respiration and heat is released as a by product.

## Slide 6

Fossil fuels contain energy that we can use to warm or light a room. Describe where this energy came from and what happens to it in our rooms.

## Slide 7


A fossil fuel is long dead organisms (mostly plants) that have been compressed into coal, oil or gas (chemical energy). This energy originally came from the sun. When the fossil fuels are used to generate electrical energy, it is then transferred into light energy that ultimately lights up the room and also heats it up.

Sunlight → ancient plants → fossil fuels → electricity → light/heat in room

## Slide 8

How are you similar to a wood-burning stove in terms of energy and matter? How are you different?

## Slide 9


Similarities - both take in chemical energy in the form of organic matter and release energy in the form of heat
Differences - in the stove the chemical energy is directly turned into heat, while in us it can be turned into other forms of chemical energy that create the structure and function of our bodies.

## Slide 10

Why are there typically more plants in a terrestrial (land) ecosystem than animals? Explain this in terms of energy.

## Slide 11

The more energy that is available at a trophic level the more organisms that trophic level can support. Because plants are producers, they capture all of the energy that is available to an ecosystem. Once the energy is transferred to animals at higher trophic levels much of it will have been lost as heat. Therefore there is less energy available to support animals.

## Slide 12

Explain the phrase: 
“Energy flows and matter cycles”

## Slide 13

Matter such as carbon, nitrogen, and other elements are part of cycles. They are transferred from one place to another, but the total amount is always the same on earth. If you followed a carbon atom long enough you would end up back in the same place it started.

Energy flows in from the sun, passes through the trophic levels and is eventually lost as heat to the universe. It does not cycle in a loop on earth like matter does.

## Slide 14

Eating “lower on the food chain” (i.e. a more plant-based diet) is generally a more efficient use of resources than eating large amounts of meat. 
Explain why this is true using the concepts of trophic levels and biological pyramids. 

## Slide 15

The amount of energy required to sustain consumers (animals) is much more than that required to sustain produces (plants). In order to eat meat you also need to grow plants to feed the animals that produce the meat. This process will always require more energy is is less efficient than eating plants directly.
