## Slide 7

In general, organisms will reproduce as much as they possibly can!
However, a population does not grow indefinitely. At some point, the population stops growing and declines as a result of one or more limiting factors
A limiting factor is a resource or environmental condition that limits a population’s growth, i.e. reduces the size or distribution of a population
Over time, there is a dynamic balance between population potential (i.e. reproduction) and limiting factors that causes population sizes to fluctuate.

## Slide 9

Carrying Capacity
Limiting factors for populations are usually resources (e.g. food, water, habitat), and there is a finite amount of these resources in any environment.

Carrying capacity = the number of organisms the environment can support over time

From your Unit Guide:
Carrying capacity refers to the number of organisms that can live sustainably in a particular environment without destroying its resources.

## Slide 10

Carrying Capacity
A population can temporarily rise above or fall below its carrying capacity.

If there are not enough resources, organisms might migrate, reproduce less, or die.
Move, change, or die!

Populations can crash, level off, or fluctuate in response to predators, seasons, etc.

## Slide 12

Ecosystem Dynamics, Functioning, and Resilience
"A complex set of interactions within an ecosystem can keep its numbers and types of organisms relatively constant over long periods of time under stable conditions.
If a modest biological or physical disturbance to an ecosystem occurs, it may return to its more or less original status (i.e., the system is resilient), as opposed to becoming a very different ecosystem.
Extreme fluctuations in conditions or the size of any population, however, can challenge the functioning of ecosystems in terms of resources and habitat availability.”

## Slide 13

In other words…
Populations naturally fluctuate, but remain relatively stable (i.e. around the carrying capacity) if the environment doesn’t change too much.

If for some reason, the environment or the population size changes dramatically, then this change can destabilize the system

In these cases, the carrying capacity itself may change indefinitely.

## Slide 14

Earth Overshoot Day
Earth Overshoot Day marks the date when humanity’s demand for ecological resources and services in a given year exceeds what Earth can regenerate in that year
